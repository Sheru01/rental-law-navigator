Evidence Debt

The unknowns are not what the system failed to answer.
They are a sourced map of where the public record cannot support an answer at all.
We built a system that refuses to guess. The by-product is an asset nobody else in the room is producing: a precise, per-jurisdiction ledger of which housing-law questions cannot be answered from public data, and exactly which missing field is the reason.
Every other team's unknowns are a scoring loss they will try to minimize. Ours are structured output. Each one already carries the rule, the jurisdiction, the specific absent fact, and the source document that proves the rule is real. Aggregate the 500 addresses and you do not get a list of failures. You get a civic data audit that no one has been able to produce before, because producing it required reading the law first.

WHY IT IS CREDIBLE FROM THIS BUILD
This is not a roadmap slide. The ledger is a grouping query over output the engine already produces. Three facts make it defensible in front of judges today.
1Every unknown already carries a structured missing fact and a reason. That is priority P2, requested for the card, and it is also the ledger's primary key.
2Unknown and indeterminate are separated, so data gaps and legal-record gaps aggregate into different columns. Priority P1 pays for itself twice.
3Every row inherits a verified span and a source id, so a city official can audit the claim that their own data is incomplete.
Build cost once the three priorities land: one aggregation and one screen. It is the cheapest slide in the deck and the only one that is a business.
HOW TO LAND IT IN 15 SECONDS
Place it at 2:40 in the 3-minute script, immediately after "what we could not do". The sequence inverts the weakness into the thesis while the judge is still holding the unknown card in mind.
"Every unknown you just saw is an entry in a ledger. Grouped by city, it says which single missing field blocks the most buildings. We set out to answer questions about housing law. What we also built, without meaning to, is a measurement of where the public record cannot answer them at all."
Then stop. Do not open a pricing slide, do not say TAM. The idea is stronger as an observation than as a pitch, and a judge who wants the business version will ask.
WHAT NOT TO CLAIM
×Do not say the data is wrong. Say the published record does not contain the field. Those are different accusations and only the second is supportable.
×Do not generalize past the sample. The ledger describes 500 addresses in a fixed corpus, on one retrieval date. Say that number out loud.
×Do not imply a city is negligent. Assessor schemas differ for ordinary historical reasons, and the ledger is useful precisely because it is neutral.
×Do not present it as built if it is not. If the aggregation does not ship, describe it as what the output makes possible and show the raw unknowns instead.
The restraint is the credibility. A system whose whole argument is that it does not overclaim cannot overclaim about itself in its closing line.
