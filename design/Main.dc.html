The design problem, stated plainly
Most teams will hide their uncertainty. The scoring rewards the opposite, and so does the law.
A missed applicable rule costs double. An honest unknown earns partial credit. So the interface must make an unknown feel like the system working, not the system breaking. Three rules follow from that, and they govern every board on this canvas.
R1Equal finish. An unknown card has the same border, padding and provenance chain as an applies card. Never a thinner, greyer, apologetic variant.
R2Name the gap. Every uncertain state states the specific missing fact and what would resolve it. Vague uncertainty reads as failure; specific uncertainty reads as rigor.
R3No alarm colors. Uncertainty is ochre and typographic, never red, never a warning triangle. Red means the system failed. Ochre means the record is thin.
The one line to say out loud
Every other system here will tell you what the law says. This one also tells you what it cannot tell you, and exactly why.
Do not say
Do not call it legal advice. Do not say the system is confident. Do not promise coverage of jurisdictions outside the corpus. Do not claim the manifest hashes verify the text; their scope is unknown and the demo should say so if asked.
Every screen carries a persistent footer: not legal advice, with the as-of date beside it. It is in the frame for all 90 seconds, never a modal the judge has to dismiss.
